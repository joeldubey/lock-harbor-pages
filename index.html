Support & User's Guide

Lock Harbor · a password manager that keeps everything on your own phone

Two things worth knowing before anything else.

1. Lock Harbor has no account and no server. Nobody — including us — can reset your master password or recover your vault. Take a backup.

2. Everything in this guide is also inside the app, under Settings → Help.

Getting started

  1. Choose a master password. This one password protects everything else, and it is the one you cannot look up. A passphrase of several unrelated words is stronger and easier to remember than a short complicated one.
  2. Save your recovery key. Lock Harbor shows a one-time key that can get you back in if you forget your master password. Write it down or store it somewhere away from the phone. You are asked to confirm parts of it so it cannot be skipped by accident.
  3. Turn on quick unlock (optional) to open the vault with a fingerprint or face instead of typing.

Your vault

The Vault tab is your home screen. Entries live in folders you create — "boat", "work", "travel" — and an entry can sit in more than one folder. Anything you have not filed shows under Unfiled.

Search works across everything regardless of folder. The filter icon beside the search box narrows to favourites or a single type, and can be used on its own without typing anything.

What you can store

  • Logins — username, password, website, notes, plus password history.
  • Secure notes — free text, encrypted like everything else.
  • Payment cards, IDs, bank accounts and Wi-Fi passwords — with the right fields for each, including masked entry for card numbers and dates.

Tap a value to reveal it; it hides itself again after 30 seconds. Copying puts it on the clipboard and clears it after a delay you control in Settings.

Making strong passwords

The Generator tab makes random passwords or passphrases and shows an honest strength estimate. Capitalisation and separators earn no credit, because an attacker knows those patterns — only real randomness counts. You can generate straight into an entry from the password field.

The Security screen

A score plus seven categories you can drill into: reused passwords, weak passwords, insecure (http://) addresses, old passwords, missing passwords, password age, and the optional breach check.

The score is a summary, not a measurement — the lists underneath are the part you can act on. A brand-new vault shows no score at all, because a vault with nothing in it is unused rather than secure.

The breach check

Off until you turn it on, because it is the only feature that uses the internet. Your password is never sent — only the first five characters of a hash of it, with the comparison done on your phone. See the Privacy Policy for exactly what that request reveals.

Filling passwords in other apps (Android)

Lock Harbor can fill saved logins into other apps and websites.

  1. Turn on Settings → Fill passwords in other apps and accept Android's confirmation.
  2. Most browsers need their own switch as well. In Chrome: ⋮ → Settings → Autofill services → Autofill using another service. Firefox needs nothing extra.

Samsung Internet is not supported yet. It asks a newer part of Android that Lock Harbor does not yet answer, so Lock Harbor will not appear there however it is configured. Use Chrome or Firefox for now.

A quick way to tell the difference: if filling works in a normal app but never in your browser, it is the browser's own setting rather than Lock Harbor.

When you tap a password field, a Lock Harbor option appears above the keyboard. Tap it, unlock if the vault is closed, and pick the entry. Only entries whose saved website matches whatever is asking are offered, and the screen always shows you who asked — check it before you tap. A look-alike address gets nothing.

If a login you expect is missing, open it in the vault and add the website address; matching runs on that.

Scanning a document

Instead of typing, you can scan:

  • Driver's licence — reads the barcode on the back. Very reliable.
  • Payment card — reads the number, expiry and cardholder name off the front, usually in under a second. The security code is never read; you always type that yourself.
  • Passport — reads the two machine-readable lines at the bottom of the photo page.

Nothing is photographed to storage — the camera image is read and discarded, and never leaves the phone. Always check the filled fields before saving. Card and passport scanning read printed text, so some fields can come back blank or wrong; embossed (raised-number) cards are the least reliable. Middle names on a passport fill about two thirds of the time.

Backups, restoring, and importing

This is the most important page in the guide. Your vault exists on one phone. If you lose it with no backup, nobody can recover your entries.

Settings → Backup creates one encrypted file containing everything, protected by a backup passphrase you choose at the time — deliberately separate from your master password, because the file has to be able to restore onto a phone that has no vault yet. Save it somewhere that survives losing the phone. You can also set a reminder to back up weekly, fortnightly or monthly.

Restoring works either on a fresh install (from the welcome screen) or into a vault you are already using. Restoring only ever adds — it never deletes or overwrites — so restoring the same file twice creates duplicates.

Importing from another manager is under Settings → Import: Bitwarden, 1Password, LastPass, Dashlane, Keeper, Chrome, NordPass, or a generic CSV. You see a preview and a per-row report of anything that could not be read before anything touches your vault. Folders are matched by name or created; individual entries are not matched against what you already have, so importing twice creates duplicates.

There is also a plain-text export. It is not protected in any way, it asks for your master password first, and it exists for moving to another manager.

Settings worth knowing

  • Lock when I leave the app — how long Lock Harbor may sit in the background before locking itself.
  • Clear copied passwords — how long a copied value stays on the clipboard.
  • Block screenshots — on by default; also hides the app in the app switcher. Turn it off temporarily if you need to capture something.
  • Autofill profile — marks one ID entry as "this is me", so new ID, card and bank entries start with your own name and address already filled in.
  • Delete everything / erase entries only — two separate actions, so you can clear your entries while keeping your master password and recovery key.
