Privacy Policy

Lock Harbor · Last updated 13 August 2026

The short version. Lock Harbor has no account, no server and no analytics. Your vault is stored encrypted on your own device and is never uploaded anywhere. We do not collect, receive, store or sell any personal information — not even an anonymous usage statistic.

There is exactly one feature that can make a network request, it is off until you switch it on, and it is described in full below.

1. What we collect

Nothing. Lock Harbor has no user accounts, no sign-in, no telemetry, no crash reporting service, no advertising identifiers and no third-party analytics. There is no server that belongs to Lock Harbor for your data to be sent to.

Everything you save — passwords, usernames, website addresses, notes, payment cards, IDs, bank accounts, Wi-Fi passwords, folder names and pictures — is stored in a single encrypted database file inside the app's private storage on your device. It stays there unless you explicitly export it yourself.

2. Your master password and encryption

Your master password is never stored, anywhere, in any form. It is used to derive a key using Argon2id, and that key protects your vault key; your entries are encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305. Titles, usernames and web addresses are inside the encrypted content rather than beside it, so the stored file contains no readable text about what you have saved.

An honest limitation, stated plainly: naming strong algorithms is not a security guarantee. Lock Harbor has not had an external security audit, and this policy makes no claim that it is unbreakable. If your master password is guessable, no amount of encryption saves it.

Nobody can recover your vault for you. Not us — we have no copy and no ability to reset anything. If you forget your master password and lose your recovery key, and you have no backup, the data is gone permanently. That is the direct cost of nothing being held on a server.

3. The one optional network request

The Security screen can check whether a password appears in publicly known data breaches, using the Have I Been Pwned Pwned Passwords service. This is off by default and asks you to confirm before it is ever enabled.

When you run a check, your password is never sent. Lock Harbor hashes it locally and sends only the first five characters of that hash, receiving back a list of many candidates and comparing them on your device. This is the standard k-anonymity method.

What that request unavoidably reveals to the service and to your network provider:

  • Your IP address, as with any web request.
  • That some password manager asked — the request itself is not secret.
  • Roughly how many distinct passwords you checked, from the number of requests.

It does not reveal which password, which account, or which website. If you never enable it, Lock Harbor makes no network requests at all and works fully in airplane mode.

4. Permissions, and why each exists

  • Camera — only to read a driver's licence barcode, a payment card, or a passport page when you tap a scan button. The image is processed on your device and never saved, uploaded, or kept after the fields are read.
  • Photos — only when you choose a picture for a folder or entry. Lock Harbor receives just the image you pick.
  • Biometrics (fingerprint / face) — optional, to unlock without typing your master password. Your biometric data never reaches the app; the operating system holds it and simply tells the app whether you passed.
  • Notifications — optional reminders about backups and old passwords. Generated on your device; no reminder names a specific entry.

Lock Harbor does not request microphone access, location, contacts, or any identifier.

5. Files you export yourself

You can create an encrypted backup, or an unencrypted plain-text export. Once you share such a file it is outside Lock Harbor's control and this policy no longer governs it — wherever you send it, that destination's rules apply. A plain-text export contains your real passwords in readable form; the app warns you and asks for your master password before producing one.

6. Children

Lock Harbor is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone, including children.

7. Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Any change that meant Lock Harbor began collecting data would be stated prominently here rather than quietly added.
