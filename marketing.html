LockHarbor

A password manager with no account, no server, and nothing to sign in to.

Your vault is encrypted and stored on your own phone. It works in airplane mode, indefinitely.

Most password managers keep a copy of your vault on their servers. That is what makes sync easy — and it is also what makes a company breach everybody's problem at once.

Lock Harbor makes the opposite trade. There is no database of users to leak, because there is no database of users. The cost is honest and worth knowing up front: nobody can recover your vault for you, so backups matter.

What it does

🔒

Everything encrypted

Argon2id protects your master password; XChaCha20-Poly1305 protects your entries. Titles and usernames sit inside the encrypted content, not beside it — the stored file holds no readable text.

🔑

Generate real passwords

Random passwords and passphrases with an honest strength estimate — one that does not give credit for patterns an attacker already expects.

📁

Folders that fit your life

Group entries however you like, with an entry allowed in more than one folder. Search covers everything at once.

🛡

See what needs attention

Reused, weak, old and insecure entries surfaced in plain lists you can act on — plus an optional, opt-in check against known breaches.

✅

Fill logins in other apps

Android autofill offers only entries that match whoever is asking, always names the requester, and never fills without you choosing.

📷

Scan instead of typing

A driver's licence barcode, a payment card, or a passport page. The image is read and discarded — never saved, never uploaded.

📥

Bring your data with you

Import from Bitwarden, 1Password, LastPass, Dashlane, Keeper, Chrome or NordPass, with a preview before anything is saved.

💾

Backups you control

One encrypted file under a passphrase you choose, restorable onto a new phone. Optional reminders so you actually take one.

More item types than just logins

Payment cards, IDs and passports, bank accounts, Wi-Fi passwords and secure notes — each with the fields that actually belong to it, and masked entry for card numbers and dates.

What Lock Harbor is not, said here rather than discovered later:

  • It has not had an external security audit. It uses established, published cryptography through maintained libraries — that describes how it is built, not a guarantee. You will not see the words "military grade" here.
  • It does not sync between devices yet. One phone, plus backups you move yourself.
  • It is Android for now. iPhone support needs a separately built mechanism.
  • No built-in two-factor code generator, deliberately — keeping a password and its second factor in one vault collapses two independent factors into one.
  • Samsung Internet autofill is not supported yet. Chrome and Firefox work.
